Ah, spoon theory. Shall I explain?

Hello, I'm Squish

I live on this page and nowhere else. I don't turn up while you're using the app, I won't interrupt you halfway through logging a rotten day, and I will never ask whether you'd like help writing a letter. You came here, so you're presumably in the mood for me.

Spoon theory, briefly

Christine Miserandino came up with it in a diner while explaining lupus to a friend. She gathered up the spoons from the tables, handed them over, and said: this is what you get today. Getting dressed costs one. Cooking costs another. Being out among people costs several. When they're gone they're gone, and tomorrow's spoons are not here yet.

The spoons themselves were incidental. The point was that some people start every day with a countable amount and have to spend it on purpose, while everybody else spends without ever noticing there was a number.

What SpoonBuddy does with that

  • You say what you're doing, and SpoonBuddy prices it by how draining it is for you. Not by how draining somebody else reckons it ought to be.
  • It keeps a running balance for the day, drawn as a spoon that empties as you spend.
  • A bad night's sleep starts you lower the next morning, because it does.
  • The balance is allowed to go below zero. The app calls that borrowing from tomorrow. It happens, sometimes there's no choice about it, and you will not be told off.

Things it will never do: congratulate you for pushing through. Show you a streak you can break. Hold yesterday up as a target. Suggest that a low day says something about your character.

It's a fuel gauge. A fuel gauge has no opinions about your driving.

Why a spoon with eyes on it

The balance gauge in the app is itself a spoon, and every colour in there is already carrying information. Green through orange to red tells you where you are, and bone means empty. I'm wood, which is the one warm colour left that isn't trying to tell you anything.

My eyes don't fit my face properly. I've made my peace with that.
